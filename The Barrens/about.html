ABOUT THE BARRENS / STOCKTON SPRINGS, MAINE

About the property.

Twenty one acres in Stockton Springs, shaped by wild blueberries, coastal woods, old stone, a seasonal garden, and the work of making a home here.

21acres in Stockton Springs

08acres of wild lowbush blueberries

05miles from Penobscot Bay

02miles from Scenic Route 1

Five miles inland from Penobscot Bay, just off Route 1A on Blueberry Lane, sits The Barrens. This is our home and working homestead: twenty one acres, a wild blueberry barren, coastal woodland, old stone, and a commitment to supporting our community through the prosperity this land provides.

Eight acres are covered in native wild lowbush blueberries. The other thirteen are wooded, forming a natural boundary behind the homestead. Our seasonal market garden, farm stand and skate lounge sit near the base of the open barrens.

The property has been ours since 2022. A commercial grower managed the blueberry ground through our first four growing seasons. In 2025, we decided to take over the operation ourselves. From here forward, we are using strict organic agricultural practices with the goal of securing MOFGA Organic Certification by 2029.

There is visible history in our fields. In the mid-1980s, USDA-supported crews cleared massive glacial boulders from the production rows and hauled them to the tree line. Those boulders still form the perimeter walls today, serving as a reminder of the hard work it took to make this field operational and the deeper historical reminder of the long history tied to this region.

We are carrying that working tradition forward through cutting, burning, hand raking, and winnowing. Our market garden vegetable and pumpkin plots remain intentionally small, guided by what the ground can support and what each Maine season allows.

Everything we do here is deliberate, including how the farm shares its success. The land gives back twice: first through the wild berries grown here for guests to harvest, and again through The Barrens Foundation.

We direct 10 percent of net profits from the U Pick blueberry experience and 1 percent of all other net profits from the Farm Stand toward local grants, community projects, and kids' skate initiatives in Maine. The land provides for us, and sharing in its success with the community is deeply rooted in how we operate.
